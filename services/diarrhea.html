Understanding Pediatric Digestion

Expert Care for Pediatric Diarrhea

When loose stools linger, identifying the root cause protects your child's growth, hydration, and comfort.

Diarrhea in children is defined as passing three or more loose or watery stools within a single day. While most childhood diarrhea episodes are acute viral stomach bugs that clear up within a week, diarrhea that persists for more than 14 days—or becomes chronic over more than four weeks—signals a deeper digestive disruption.

Prolonged loose stools can lead to dehydration, electrolyte imbalances, painful diaper dermatitis, and poor weight gain. Whether caused by carbohydrate malabsorption, cow's milk protein allergy, post-viral mucosal blunting, or inflammatory bowel disease, our pediatric gastroenterologists pinpoint the precise mechanism to restore comfortable, normal digestion.

Categories of Pediatric Diarrhea

Acute Diarrhea (<14 Days)

Sudden-onset watery stools typically sparked by viral gastroenteritis (rotavirus, norovirus) or food poisoning, requiring careful hydration monitoring.

Persistent Diarrhea (14–28 Days)

Subacute loose stools often reflecting post-infectious lactase deficiency or secondary mucosal injury that hasn't fully healed.

Chronic Diarrhea (>4 Weeks)

Ongoing loose bowel movements indicating toddler's diarrhea, celiac disease, food allergies, IBD, or small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO).

Diagnostic Clarity

Common Causes of Children's Diarrhea

Pediatric loose stools can stem from dietary imbalances, enzyme deficits, or immune-mediated gut inflammation. Our specialists systematically differentiate between them.

Toddler's Diarrhea (Dietary Osmotic)

Excessive consumption of fruit juices, sorbitol, or high-sugar drinks paired with low healthy fat intake, speeding intestinal transit in healthy, thriving young children.

Carbohydrate & Lactose Intolerance

Temporary or permanent lactase enzyme deficiency following an infection, or congenital sucrose intolerance causing explosive, acidic stools, gas, and perianal redness.

Celiac Disease & Food Allergies

Immune-mediated reactions to dietary gluten or cow's milk protein (allergic colitis) that cause blunted intestinal villi, nutrient malabsorption, and chronic loose stools.

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis causing chronic digestive tract inflammation, frequent bloody diarrhea, nocturnal waking, and weight loss.

Persistent Infections & Parasites

Lingering pathogens such as Giardia, Clostridioides difficile, Cryptosporidium, or Salmonella requiring targeted stool testing and specific antimicrobial treatments.

Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO)

An abnormal proliferation of colon-type bacteria within the small intestine, leading to early fermentation, bloating, foul-smelling loose stools, and vitamin deficiencies.

Warning Signs for Parents

When to Seek Immediate Medical Evaluation

Because infants and young children can dehydrate rapidly, certain red flag symptoms indicate that pediatric diarrhea requires urgent medical evaluation:

No urination or dry diapers for more than 6 to 8 hours
Crying without producing tears, or extreme dry mouth
Visible bright red blood or black tarry stools
High fevers (>102°F) or persistent vomiting preventing fluids
Unexplained weight loss or falling across growth percentiles
Lethargy, unusual confusion, or difficulty waking up

Diagnostic Excellence

How Diarrhea Is Evaluated at PEDGI

We prioritize thoughtful, non-invasive testing first to quickly identify whether the diarrhea is osmotic, secretory, inflammatory, or malabsorptive.

1
Comprehensive Dietary & Growth Assessment

Detailed analysis of daily beverage intake (juices, milk, sports drinks), recent antibiotic usage, travel history, and longitudinal growth velocity tracking.

2
Targeted Stool & Calprotectin Biomarkers

PCR testing for parasites and enteric bacteria, fecal reducing substances (measuring unabsorbed sugars), fecal elastase (pancreatic health), and calprotectin (inflammation).

3
Celiac Disease & Nutritional Blood Screening

Tissue transglutaminase (tTG-IgA) testing to accurately screen for celiac disease, alongside complete blood count, electrolytes, and serum albumin levels.

4
Hydrogen Breath Testing & Endoscopy (When Needed)

Non-invasive breath tests for lactose/fructose malabsorption or SIBO. For suspected inflammatory conditions, pediatric upper endoscopy and colonoscopy are performed under gentle pediatric sedation.

Evidence-Based Care

Pediatric Diarrhea Treatment & Management

Effective treatment focuses on rehydration, restoring mucosal integrity, and modifying dietary triggers tailored to your child's diagnosis.

Oral Rehydration Therapy

Targeted electrolyte and glucose rehydration solutions formulated specifically to prevent dehydration without aggravating osmotic fluid shifts.

Dietary Optimization

Eliminating concentrated fruit juices and sorbitol, balancing healthy fats and dietary fiber, or starting temporary lactose-free nutrition.

Targeted Medical Therapy

Enzyme replacement therapy (such as Sucraid for sucrose intolerance or pancreatic enzymes), specific antibiotics for SIBO, or anti-inflammatory drugs for IBD.

Ongoing Monitoring

Close follow-up of growth trajectories, stool resolution, and perianal skin healing with our doctors and Physician Extenders.

Pediatric Subspecialty Focus

Toddler's Diarrhea vs. Malabsorption: What Parents Need to Know

One of the most reassuring insights for parents is distinguishing between benign rapid transit and true nutrient malabsorption.

In toddlers, the condition known as "toddler's diarrhea" (chronic nonspecific diarrhea) is common. Stools are loose and may contain recognizable peas, carrots, or corn. However, the child is bright, energetic, smiling, and gaining weight normally along their CDC growth chart. In contrast, malabsorptive diarrhea is accompanied by weight loss, muscle wasting, greasy foul stools, or severe lethargy. Our pediatric specialists quickly bring clarity to your family.

Frequently Asked Questions

Diarrhea Questions Answered

Have questions about your child's loose stools, hydration, or toddler's diarrhea? Here are answers from our pediatric GI team.

View All General FAQs
When does diarrhea in a child become "chronic"?

Acute diarrhea typically lasts less than 7 to 14 days and is commonly caused by viral stomach bugs. Diarrhea is clinically classified as persistent when it continues between 2 and 4 weeks, and chronic when loose, watery stools persist for more than 4 weeks. Chronic diarrhea requires comprehensive evaluation by a pediatric gastroenterologist.

What is "toddler's diarrhea" and is it dangerous?

Toddler's diarrhea (chronic nonspecific diarrhea of childhood) is a benign motility condition common between ages 1 and 3. Children pass multiple loose, watery stools during the day—often containing undigested food fragments—but remain happy, energetic, and continue growing along their normal growth curves. It is typically resolved through dietary balancing.

Can fruit juices or sweetened drinks cause loose stools in toddlers?

Yes, very frequently. Apple juice, pear juice, and sweetened drinks contain high amounts of fructose and sorbitol. Young digestive tracts cannot absorb excessive simple sugars, creating an osmotic pull that draws water into the large intestine and causes watery stools.

What are the earliest signs of dehydration in an infant or child with diarrhea?

Key signs of pediatric dehydration include crying without tears, dry lips or sticky mouth, sunken eyes or sunken soft spot (fontanelle) in infants, lethargy or unusually high fussiness, and going more than 6 to 8 hours without producing a wet diaper or urinating.

Should I give my child over-the-counter anti-diarrhea medicine (like loperamide)?

No. Over-the-counter anti-diarrheal medications are not recommended for infants and young children unless specifically prescribed by a physician. They can paralyze intestinal motility and cause dangerous complications. The focus must be on oral electrolyte rehydration and finding the underlying medical cause.

How quickly can my child be seen at PEDGI for persistent diarrhea?

PEDGI prioritizes children suffering from lingering diarrhea, stomach pain, or suspected malabsorption. Urgent requests are typically scheduled within 2 to 5 business days across our four Tampa Bay clinic locations. View our Insurance Page for coverage details.
