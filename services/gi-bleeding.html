Understanding Pediatric GI Bleeding

Expert Care for Pediatric & Adolescent GI Bleeding

Identifying the source of gastrointestinal bleeding in your child or teenager brings clarity, peace of mind, and effective relief to the whole family.

Gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding in children and teenagers occurs when blood loss develops anywhere along the digestive tract, from the esophagus and stomach down to the small intestine, colon, and rectum. Causes vary significantly by age, ranging from constipation fissures and milk protein allergies in infants to juvenile polyps or Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) in adolescents.

Children and teenagers experience GI bleeding symptoms differently. Infants may show bloody streaks in diapers, while older children and teenagers report abdominal cramping, dark tarry stools, or blood in the toilet. A dedicated pediatric gastroenterologist for GI bleeding provides the subspecialty care needed to identify the exact cause and create a safe treatment plan.

Diagnostic Clarity

Common Causes of Pediatric & Teen GI Bleeding

GI bleeding in children and teenagers stems from anatomical, inflammatory, or infectious factors. Our pediatric GI specialists thoroughly evaluate each underlying trigger.

Anal Fissures & Constipation

Minor superficial tears in the anal lining caused by passing hard stools, frequent in toddlers and young children.

Allergic Colitis (Milk Protein)

Immune response to cow's milk or soy proteins causing mild rectal bleeding and loose stools in infants.

Juvenile Colonic Polyps

Benign tissue growths in the colon occurring in children aged 2–10, causing painless bright red rectal bleeding.

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

Crohn's Disease and Ulcerative Colitis causing chronic intestinal inflammation, bloody diarrhea, and weight loss in teens.

Peptic Ulcers & Esophagitis

Mucosal erosion in the stomach, duodenum, or esophagus caused by stomach acid, H. pylori, or medication irritation.

Infectious Colitis

Bacterial or viral gastroenteritis (such as Salmonella or E. coli) leading to acute intestinal inflammation and bloody stools.

Warning Signs for Parents

When to Seek Immediate Medical Evaluation

While minor blood streaks from constipation can be evaluated during clinic hours, certain "red flag" symptoms indicate that children's and teens' GI bleeding requires immediate medical care:

Vomiting bright red blood or dark coffee-ground material
Passing large amounts of bright red blood or maroon stools
Dark, black, tarry-smelling stools (melena)
Dizziness, extreme pale skin, weakness, or fainting
Severe, sudden, or persistent abdominal pain
Unexplained weight loss, fever, or chronic anemia in teens

How GI Bleeding Is Evaluated at PEDGI

We believe in a thoughtful, step-by-step diagnostic journey tailored specifically for children and teenagers. Our gentle approach focuses on non-invasive assessments first.

1
Child & Teen Clinical History

Detailed review of stool appearance, feeding/dietary habits, medication history, and growth trends.

2
Non-Invasive Stool & Blood Work

Checking stool calprotectin, occult blood, inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP), and complete blood count for anemia.

3
Pediatric Upper Endoscopy (EGD) & Colonoscopy

Visualizing GI mucosa, taking biopsies, or removing polyps under gentle pediatric sedation managed by pediatric anesthesiologists.

4
Wireless Pill Capsule Endoscopy

In older children and teens, wireless video capsule endoscopy allows non-invasive visualization of the small intestine.

Frequently Asked Questions

GI Bleeding Questions Answered

Have questions about gastrointestinal bleeding or preparing for a specialist visit? Here are common answers from our pediatric GI team.

View All General FAQs
What causes blood in a child's or teenager's stool?

Causes vary significantly by age. In infants, milk protein allergy or diaper dermatitis are common. In school-aged children, constipation fissures, bacterial infections, or benign juvenile polyps are frequent. In teenagers and young adults, Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Crohn’s Disease or Ulcerative Colitis) and peptic ulcers are major considerations.

Is blood in stool an emergency for a child or teenager?

Minor streaks of bright red blood on toilet paper from hard bowel movements can usually be evaluated promptly during regular clinic hours. However, passing large volumes of blood, dark tarry stools, vomiting blood, feeling dizzy/weak, or experiencing severe abdominal pain requires immediate emergency medical evaluation.

What is the difference between bright red blood and dark tarry stool?

Bright red blood (hematochezia) usually indicates bleeding in the lower GI tract, such as the rectum or colon. Dark, black, tarry stool (melena) indicates blood digested from upper GI sources like the esophagus, stomach, or duodenum.

Does PEDGI specialize in gastroenterology for teenagers up to age 21?

Yes! PEDGI provides specialized pediatric gastroenterology care for patients from birth through age 21. We understand the unique physical, emotional, and social needs of teenagers and young adults, providing respectful, individualized care.

How are GI endoscopic procedures performed safely in children and teens?

When endoscopy or colonoscopy is indicated, procedures are performed in dedicated pediatric facility settings under gentle pediatric sedation or general anesthesia managed by experienced pediatric anesthesiologists.

How quickly can my child or teenager be seen at PEDGI for GI bleeding?

PEDGI prioritizes urgent gastrointestinal bleeding concerns. Non-emergent GI bleeding evaluations are typically scheduled within 24 to 48 hours across our 4 Tampa Bay clinic locations.
