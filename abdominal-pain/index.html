Understanding Pediatric Stomach Pain

Expert Care for Pediatric Abdominal Pain

When your child complains of belly aches, getting the right diagnosis brings peace of mind to your entire family.

Abdominal pain in children is one of the most frequent reasons parents seek medical guidance. Stomach discomfort can range from brief, self-limiting episodes to chronic or recurrent aches that disrupt school, sleep, and physical activities.

Children's digestive systems are growing and developing constantly. Unlike adults, young children often struggle to describe the exact location or type of pain they feel. A dedicated pediatric gastroenterologist for abdominal pain possesses the specialized clinical training and child-friendly diagnostic tools necessary to identify the root cause and restore your child's comfort.

Acute vs. Chronic Stomach Pain in Kids

Acute Abdominal Pain

Sudden-onset pain lasting hours or a few days. Often tied to temporary infections, dietary changes, or acute conditions requiring prompt medical checkups.

Recurrent & Chronic Pain

Stomach discomfort occurring at least once a week over a period of 2 months or more. Requires comprehensive pediatric GI evaluation.

Functional & Organic Etiologies

Distinguishing structural conditions (like GERD or Celiac) from gut-brain sensitivity to tailor an effective, individual treatment plan.

Diagnostic Clarity

Common Causes of Children's Stomach Pain

Pediatric stomach pain stems from a variety of gastrointestinal, dietary, or functional factors. Our specialists thoroughly investigate each potential trigger.

Functional GI Disorders (FGD)

Functional Abdominal Pain, Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), and Functional Dyspepsia where gut nerve sensitivity causes recurrent pain without structural damage.

Acid Reflux & GERD

Stomach acid flowing back into the esophagus, causing upper belly burning, regurgitation, nausea, or chest discomfort in children and teens.

Constipation & Motility Issues

Infrequent or difficult bowel movements leading to cramped abdominal distress, bloating, and decreased appetite in young patients.

Food Intolerances & Celiac Disease

Lactose intolerance, milk protein sensitivity, or autoimmune reactions to gluten that cause cramping, gas, diarrhea, and poor growth.

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis causing chronic digestive inflammation, weight loss, persistent diarrhea, and severe abdominal cramping.

Infections & Inflammation

Viral or bacterial gastroenteritis, stomach bugs, gastritis, or organ inflammation requiring accurate diagnosis and targeted treatment.

Warning Signs for Parents

When to Seek Immediate Medical Care

While many belly aches resolve quickly, certain "red flag" symptoms indicate that pediatric abdominal pain requires urgent evaluation by a healthcare professional or pediatric GI doctor:

Severe, sudden pain or pain localized to the lower right side
High fever or persistent vomiting (especially green/yellow bile)
Blood in vomit or dark, bloody stool
Pain that consistently wakes your child from sleep
Unexplained weight loss or poor physical growth
Joint swelling, rash, or persistent lethargy

Specialized Focus Area

Understanding Functional Abdominal Pain (FAP)

One of the most reassuring facts for parents to learn is that severe, recurring stomach pain does not always mean there is permanent organ damage.

Functional Abdominal Pain (FAP) occurs when the nerves of the digestive tract become overly sensitive or miscommunicate with the brain—a condition known as visceral hypersensitivity. The pain experienced by the child is 100% real, but standard diagnostic tests often appear completely normal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Abdominal Pain Questions Answered

Have questions about your child's stomach pain or preparing for a specialist visit? Here are common answers from our pediatric GI team.

View All General FAQs
How do I know if my child's stomach pain is serious?

Stomach pain in children should be evaluated by a specialist if it is severe, persistent, wakes your child from sleep, or occurs alongside red flag symptoms such as fever, persistent vomiting, blood in stool, unexplainable weight loss, or pain localized to the lower right abdomen.

What is functional abdominal pain in children?

Functional abdominal pain (FAP) refers to real abdominal pain caused by heightened nerve sensitivity or altered communication between the digestive tract and brain, rather than structural damage or disease. It is one of the most common causes of chronic stomach pain in children and responds very well to specialized GI management.

When should we consult a pediatric gastroenterologist for abdominal pain?

You should consult a pediatric gastroenterologist when your child experiences stomach pain lasting more than two weeks, pain that interferes with school or daily activities, recurrent belly aches without a clear cause, or when primary pediatric treatments haven't brought relief.

Will my child need an endoscopy or invasive testing?

Not necessarily. Most pediatric abdominal pain evaluations begin with non-invasive steps such as a complete medical history, physical examination, laboratory tests, or abdominal ultrasound. Advanced procedures like upper endoscopy (EGD) or colonoscopy are only recommended when clinically necessary.

How quickly can my child be seen at PEDGI?

PEDGI prioritizes timely care for uncomfortable children. Urgent requests are typically seen within 2 to 5 business days across our four Tampa Bay clinic locations.
